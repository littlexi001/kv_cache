\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Long-context language models promise document retrieval, multi-hop reasoning,
and persistent agent memory through a simple interface: place the relevant
material in the prompt and let attention recover it when needed.  Context
windows have accordingly grown from thousands to hundreds of thousands of
tokens, and rotary position embeddings (RoPE) have become a standard way to
encode order inside those windows~\citep{su2024roformer}.  Yet accepting a long
sequence is not the same as using it reliably.  Accuracy still depends on where
evidence appears, how much irrelevant text surrounds it, and which retrieval
pattern is required~\citep{liu2024lost,bai2024longbench,hsieh2024ruler}.

We study a sharper contradiction: \emph{the semantic evidence--query relation
can remain fixed while the answer changes abruptly as their separation and
surrounding context change}.  In one controlled, position-extended Qwen3-8B
age-retrieval diagnostic using YaRN, two prompts share the same gold evidence
sentence and terminal question, but the second extends the intervening body by
64 tokens from the same fixed distractor/filler stream.  The correct first-token probability falls from
42.62\% to 1.75\%, and its margin against a formatting token changes from
$+1.00$ to $-2.75$.  Dense length sweeps show that correct and incorrect regions
can alternate.  The failure is
therefore not adequately described by a monotone ``context rot'' curve: we need
to identify which computation first changes, how that change propagates, and
which internal quantity actually determines whether evidence helps the answer.

\textbf{RoPE makes content matching phase-sensitive.}
RoPE rotates each two-dimensional query/key pair at a different effective
frequency.  Let $\kappa$ be the implementation's multiplicative rotary scale
on each of Q and K ($\kappa=1$ for unscaled RoPE).  For fixed pre-RoPE content
vectors, pair $i$ contributes at relative distance $\Delta$
\begin{equation}
  s_i(\Delta)
  = \kappa^2\!\left[A_i\cos(\Delta\omega_i)+B_i\sin(\Delta\omega_i)\right].
  \label{eq:intro-phase}
\end{equation}
The score is thus a finite mixture of distance-dependent phases, not a monotone
penalty.  Summing all 64 analytic pair contributions reconstructs measured
first-layer QK scores with at most $1.98\times10^{-3}$ absolute error while the
pre-RoPE query and key remain identical.  This establishes the first link:
position alone can change the admission score of fixed semantic evidence.

\textbf{Attention amount is not evidence utility.}
A lower score usually reduces a token's softmax probability, but probability
only says how much the token is read.  It does not say what the corresponding
Value writes or whether that write supports the correct answer.  We derive an
exact local decomposition in which the effect of score $s_j$ on the
gold--conflict margin is the product of three terms: attention probability,
the token's Value relative to the current attention-weighted Value mean, and
the downstream margin gradient.  This distinction resolves an otherwise
important counterexample: across seed-macro means, plausible conflict and gold
evidence show no reliable difference in attention probability or grid-envelope
RoPE suppression, yet their average answer-directed score utilities have different
answer-level roles.  This is a failure to detect a mean gap, not an equivalence
test or a claim that individual tokens are unclassifiable.

\textbf{No-op-matched interventions close the local causal step.}
We increase one pre-softmax score by $0.25$ and compare the resulting margin
change with its first-order prediction on exactly the same instrumented BF16
graph.  For oracle-selected evidence coordinates in Qwen3-8B, Pearson
correlations are 0.960/0.973/0.936 and Spearman correlations are
0.947/0.976/0.900 at 8K/16K/24K; the predicted sign is correct for
15/16, 15/16, and 13/16 events.  Every length uses 8 independent seeds and a
seed-cluster bootstrap.  Exact residual replay and a separate high-resolution
activation-patching case then support the propagation step: a first-layer
attention difference enters the residual stream, changes later pre-RoPE
queries, and can reverse the final decision when patched.

\textbf{The analysis also limits what a repair can claim.}
For a fixed query, support, and Value set, any token-specific phase change is
functionally identical to adding the induced attention-logit bias.  Under a
single reusable GQA cache, rotary phase corrections are more constrained: they
must separate into a cached Key-side phase and a Query-side phase, equivalently
satisfying a vanishing query--key four-cycle condition.  A repair that violates
this condition requires Key recomputation, multiple cached bases, or an
explicit pairwise score edit.  We therefore treat pre-RoPE retrieval only as a
diagnostic intervention already adjacent to prior sparse-retrieval systems,
not as the paper's main novelty~\citep{mu2025sals,lu2024longheads,xiao2024infllm}.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We isolate non-monotone, distance-sensitive retrieval failures and
  reconstruct the first RoPE layer at the frequency-pair level.
  \item We distinguish attention amount from answer-directed score utility and
  validate the local score-to-margin derivative with no-op-matched
  interventions across three context lengths.
  \item We connect the local effect to later computation through exact residual
  replay and activation patching, while stating which mediation links remain
  unmeasured.
  \item We characterize the functional identifiability and GQA-cache
  realizability boundaries of query-specific phase repair.
\end{enumerate}

The evidence is intentionally scoped.  It triangulates adjacent mechanism
links across distinct controlled Qwen3-8B protocols rather than treating them
as one same-example mediation experiment.  It does not yet establish
universality, full-path mediation proportions, or a deployable new positional
encoding.

\begin{abstract}
Long context windows can contain the right evidence without making it reliably
usable.  We study controlled retrieval failures in which evidence and query
wording are fixed while context length changes, jointly varying their separation
and competing context.  In a position-extended,
YaRN-scaled Qwen3-8B diagnostic, extending the shared context by 64 tokens can move the correct
first-token probability from 42.62\% to 1.75\%, yet dense sweeps reveal
oscillatory success and failure rather than monotone decay.  We trace this
behavior through the model.  At the first layer,
a fixed pre-RoPE query--key match decomposes exactly into 64 sinusoidal frequency
pairs; the reconstruction error is at most $1.98\times10^{-3}$.  However, low
post-RoPE score or attention mass alone does not determine whether a token helps
the answer.  We derive the directed local utility of a score as the product of
its attention probability, its centered Value direction, and the downstream
answer-margin gradient.  In no-op-matched BF16 singleton interventions at 8K,
16K, and 24K, this derivative predicts the measured margin change on evidence
coordinates with Pearson correlations 0.960/0.973/0.936 and Spearman
correlations 0.947/0.976/0.900.  Across seed-macro means, we detect no reliable
gold-minus-conflict gap in attention probability or grid-envelope RoPE
suppression, whereas answer-directed score utility has a positive gap.  Exact residual
replay and activation patching support the subsequent cross-layer propagation.
Finally, we prove
that query-specific phase repair is functionally a logit edit and characterize
when a rotary repair is realizable by one reusable GQA KV cache.  The result is
a scoped mechanism account---phase modulates attention score; local utility
jointly depends on attention probability, centered Value direction, and the
downstream gradient; and nonlinear residual dynamics propagate the
difference---rather than a claim of a mature new positional encoding.
These links are triangulated across distinct controlled protocols; we do not
present them as a same-example estimate of full-path mediation.
\end{abstract}

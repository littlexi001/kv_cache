\begin{abstract}
Long context windows expose a language model to more information, but do not
ensure that semantically relevant evidence receives retrieval priority.  We
study a controlled failure in which the evidence and query contents are fixed
while their separation changes.  In a position-extended Qwen3-8B, adding only
64 filler tokens can change the probability of the correct first answer token
from 42.62\% to 1.75\%.  We trace this failure from the first attention layer to
the output.  For a fixed pre-RoPE query--key pair, the post-RoPE score is a sum
of sinusoidal terms over relative distance; our exact decomposition
reconstructs measured first-layer scores with at most $1.98\times10^{-3}$
absolute error.  The resulting attention perturbation enters the residual
stream, grows across layers, and changes the answer margin; activation patching
at layer 16 is already sufficient to reverse the failed decision.  This
analysis motivates \method, which preserves standard RoPE for local structure
and final attention computation, but uses a position-free pre-RoPE score to
propose remote candidates before a sparse softmax.  On a held-out controlled
two-hop retrieval suite with 24 seeds per length, 8K--64K contexts, and a 2\%
candidate budget, the conservative \methodpost variant reduces aggregate
geometric \GoldPPL from 10.851 under full attention to 4.859 and raises
first-token accuracy from 33.3\% to 47.9\%.  At 16K and 32K it also improves
paired NLL over exact post-RoPE Top-2\% selection; the 64K interval crosses zero.
These results establish a mechanism and a targeted repair in a controlled
retrieval regime, not yet a universal long-context solution: evaluation on
natural benchmarks, additional architectures, and an approximate retrieval
index remain open.
\end{abstract}
